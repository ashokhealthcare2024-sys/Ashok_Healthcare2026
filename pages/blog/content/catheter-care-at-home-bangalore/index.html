Catheter Care at Home: Essential Hygiene, Safety, and Infection Prevention

Written by Ashok Healthcare clinical team · Last updated: 2026-11-11

Written by Ashok Healthcare clinical team | Last updated: 2026-11-11

Returning home with an indwelling urinary catheter can raise questions for patients and families. A catheter and its drainage bag need careful everyday handling, and any change to the device or care routine should be guided by the patient's clinician.

This article is general education only. It does not replace the patient's written care plan or instruction from a qualified nurse or doctor.

Understand the closed drainage system

An indwelling urinary catheter drains urine through tubing into a collection bag. Keeping the system closed and the tubing positioned correctly helps reduce contamination and supports drainage. The treating team should explain the patient's specific device and when a clinician needs to review or replace it.

  • Do not disconnect the catheter from its drainage tubing unless a clinician instructs you.
  • Keep the bag below bladder level and off the floor.
  • Check that tubing is not trapped under the patient or sharply kinked.
  • Follow the nurse's instructions for emptying and cleaning the collection bag.

Everyday hygiene and handling

Wash hands before and after touching the catheter or bag. Keep the area around the catheter clean as instructed by the nurse, and use only products recommended for the patient. Avoid pulling or tugging on the tubing; ask the nurse how to secure it comfortably and safely.

Do not flush, remove, reinsert or change a catheter at home unless a qualified clinician has explicitly instructed and trained you to do so. If the catheter slips out, do not push it back in; contact the clinical team promptly.

Preventing catheter-associated infection

Consistent hand hygiene and an intact drainage connection are important general precautions. Contact the treating clinician if the patient develops fever, chills, new discomfort, blood in the urine, unusual urine appearance or smell, or any other concerning change. These signs need clinical assessment; do not start antibiotics or other treatment without a clinician's advice.

Fluid needs differ from person to person. Follow the treating doctor's advice, especially if the patient has heart, kidney or other conditions that affect fluid intake. Do not increase fluids based only on general online guidance.

Safe drainage bag positioning

Keep the bag lower than the bladder whether the patient is in bed, sitting or moving, and make sure it does not rest on the floor. Check the tubing for twists or kinks and follow the nurse's instructions for emptying the bag. Avoid touching the outlet to the container or other surfaces while emptying; ask the nurse to demonstrate the process for the specific bag.

When to contact a clinician urgently

Contact the nurse or doctor promptly if urine stops draining, the catheter comes out, the patient has new lower abdominal discomfort, or urine appears bloody or unusually cloudy. Do not try to flush or replace the catheter yourself.

Seek urgent medical help for severe pain, fever with chills, heavy bleeding, confusion, severe weakness or a rapidly worsening condition. Call 108 in a medical emergency.

Home nurse or attendant?

A trained attendant can assist with everyday personal care within their training. Catheter insertion, replacement, removal, flushing and clinical assessment should be performed by an appropriately qualified nurse or doctor. Ask your care provider about professional credentials, the patient's care plan and who to contact if a problem occurs.

Frequently asked questions

How often should a catheter be changed?

Timing depends on the catheter type and the patient's condition. Follow the schedule set by the treating clinician; catheter changes should be handled by a qualified professional.

What can cause urine to leak around the catheter?

There are several possible causes, including blockage or bladder irritation. Contact the nurse or doctor for advice rather than trying to adjust or flush the catheter.

Can a patient shower with a urinary catheter?

Ask the treating clinician whether showering is appropriate and how to keep the device secure. Follow the care team's hygiene instructions.

Can we use tap or borewell water for catheter care?

Use the cleaning method and products recommended by the patient's nurse or doctor. Do not use water or antiseptics on the catheter system unless instructed.

How much water should a catheterized patient drink?

Fluid needs vary by patient. Follow the treating doctor's guidance, particularly if the patient has kidney or heart disease or a prescribed fluid restriction.

What is the difference between a home nurse and an attendant?

A qualified nurse can provide clinical care within their training and scope. An attendant helps with non-clinical daily activities and should not perform catheter procedures.

Need support with catheter care at home?

Contact our team to ask about home nursing support. Keep the patient's treating doctor informed about any changes or concerns.
