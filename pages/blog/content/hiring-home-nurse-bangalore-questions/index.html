What to Ask When Hiring a Home Nurse in Bangalore

A home nurse may become an important part of a patient's recovery or long-term care. Asking clear questions before service starts helps the family understand qualifications, responsibilities, communication, and how the care plan will be followed.

Written by Ashok Healthcare clinical team · Last updated: 2026-11-11

Disclaimer: This guide is general information, not medical or legal advice. Verify current credentials and service terms directly, and follow the treating clinician's plan.

Clarify the care need before searching

Begin with the discharge summary or current care instructions. List the tasks the patient needs, the times they are needed, mobility limits, devices in use, and who is responsible for medical decisions. Ask the treating clinician which tasks require a nurse and which can be handled by family or a non-clinical attendant. Describe changes in the patient's condition accurately; do not assume that a routine nursing shift is appropriate for a person whose health is unstable or rapidly deteriorating.

Decide whether support is required for a few visits, a day or night shift, or longer coverage. Include the patient's preferences, communication needs, language, privacy, and family routines. A realistic task list helps the provider match staff experience and quote the correct arrangement. Tell the provider about pets, stairs, equipment, and other practical details that could affect safe work in the home.

Ask about credentials and role boundaries

Ask what nursing qualification the assigned person holds and how the provider verifies it. Confirm the person's identity and any current registration or documentation relevant to the role. Ask whether qualifications can be checked through the appropriate council or issuing body. If the patient needs a specialized task—such as airway, catheter, feeding tube, wound, or ventilator-related care—ask how the provider assesses competency for that exact duty and whether the person has recent experience with it.

Ask which tasks are included and which are outside the nurse's scope. Clinical procedures should follow a valid prescription and the treating team's instructions. Clarify whether the nurse can administer medicines, perform a dressing, monitor specified observations, or assist with a device, and how each task will be documented. Do not rely on vague statements such as “handles everything”; request a patient-specific description and ask the treating clinician to confirm the plan.

Understand assessment, handover, and documentation

Ask whether the provider reviews the discharge summary, prescriptions, and home environment before assigning staff. Find out how the nurse receives the patient's history, allergies, current medicines, emergency contacts, and precautions. The family should share only necessary information through an appropriate channel and keep the latest care instructions available. Confirm that the assigned nurse can ask questions before beginning unfamiliar tasks.

Ask what records are kept: for example, medicines given, observations requested by the doctor, intake/output if prescribed, concerns, and handover notes. Find out who reviews those records and how they can be shared with the treating doctor. Documentation should be factual and should not substitute for medical review. Ask how family members can report an error or correct information in a record.

Ask who is contacted when something changes

Before the first shift, agree on a clear escalation pathway. Ask which number the nurse calls for a clinical concern, who is the treating doctor, how after-hours support works, and when emergency services should be contacted. Clarify what the nurse can decide independently and what requires a doctor. Do not assume that a provider's coordination line is the same as a 24-hour doctor service; verify the support that is actually available.

Ask how the provider handles staff absence, late arrival, or a request for replacement. Confirm expected notification, who arranges cover, and whether a replacement with matching competencies can be guaranteed. Do not rely on an unverified response-time promise. If continuous coverage is essential, ask the provider to describe how breaks, rest, and handovers are arranged and identify any periods when a family member must take over.

Check shift terms and continuity

Request the shift schedule, start date, minimum booking period, weekly rest arrangements, holidays, overtime policy, cancellation terms, and payment dates in writing. Ask whether the same nurse will attend each shift, whether the provider can introduce a backup, and how the family will be informed about changes. Clarify any costs for assessment, travel, supplies, or urgent start requests. Compare providers using the same description of duties and hours so the estimates are meaningful.

Ask how the nurse will be introduced to the patient and family and whether an initial handover or orientation is included. A short overlap can help explain routines and reduce missed information. If the patient has a language or communication preference, discuss how it will be handled. Tell the provider promptly if the patient's needs or behaviour change, because an assignment suitable at the start may need review later.

Discuss privacy, safety, and concerns

Ask how the provider screens and identifies staff, protects patient information, and handles photographs or records. Make sure the patient knows who is entering the home and how to raise a concern privately. Keep medicines, valuables, and records in an agreed place. Establish safe boundaries for visitors and household members, and agree on who may discuss care decisions with the provider.

Find out the complaint process and expected response route. Ask who supervises the assignment, how often the provider checks in, and how the family can request a change. If something feels unsafe, report it and contact the treating clinician. In an immediate emergency, seek emergency help rather than waiting for routine agency follow-up.

A practical question list for the first call

Families can keep a short checklist: What qualification and experience does the assigned nurse have for this patient's needs? Which tasks are included? How is competency checked? What is the handover process? What records are kept? Who answers clinical concerns after hours? How are absence and replacement handled? What are the full shift and cancellation terms? What information should the family prepare? What does the provider need from the treating doctor before care begins?

Write down the answers and ask for them in a message or service agreement. A professional provider should explain limitations as well as capabilities. If a representative cannot answer a clinical-scope question, ask for a qualified person to clarify it instead of accepting guesswork. Choose a service arrangement that fits the treating clinician's plan and the patient's preferences.
