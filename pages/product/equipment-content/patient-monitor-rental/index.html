What does a multipara monitor measure?

A multipara (multi-parameter) monitor measures several vital signs at once, typically ECG and heart rate, oxygen saturation (SpO2), non-invasive blood pressure, breathing rate and temperature. It displays them on one screen and gives audible and visual alarms when a reading goes outside limits set according to the doctor's advice.

A monitor is only useful if someone is watching and knows how to respond. Monitors are usually rented alongside a trained nurse, particularly for home ICU patients.

Frequently asked questions

Who watches the monitor at home?

Ideally, a trained nurse who understands the readings and alarms. Families can learn the basics, but clinical decisions should involve a nurse or doctor.

Does the monitor work during a power cut?

The monitor has an internal battery for backup. The duration depends on the device and operating conditions; ask our team about the available model.

Are the readings as accurate as hospital monitors?

Readings depend on correct sensor placement and a still patient. Unusual readings should be rechecked and discussed with the doctor.

Can the doctor see the readings remotely?

Nurses can record readings on a chart that can be shared with the doctor.

What should we do if an alarm sounds?

Check the patient first, then sensor placement. Follow the nurse's instructions and contact the doctor, or call 108 if the patient is unwell.
