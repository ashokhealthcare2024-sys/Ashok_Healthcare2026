What is palliative care at home?

Palliative care is care that focuses on relieving pain, breathlessness and other distressing symptoms, and on the person's comfort and dignity, for anyone living with a serious or life-limiting illness. It can be given alongside treatment, not only at the end of life. At home, it is delivered by nurses following the plan made by the patient's doctor or palliative care team.

People often think of palliative care only in the last days of life. In practice, it can help at many stages of illnesses such as advanced cancer, heart failure, lung disease, kidney failure, stroke and neurological conditions. The focus is on how the person feels each day: whether they are in pain, whether they can breathe comfortably, whether they are clean, rested and not alone.

Many patients prefer to be at home, among familiar people and surroundings. Our nurses make that possible by taking on the physical care, the medicines and the observations, so that family members can spend their time simply being with their loved one.

Palliative nursing care we provide

Care is coordinated with the patient's doctor or palliative care team.

Comfort and symptom support

  • Giving prescribed pain relief and other comfort medicines on time, and recording their effect.
  • Noting symptoms such as pain, breathlessness, nausea, restlessness or constipation and reporting changes to the doctor.
  • Positioning, pillows and mouth care to keep the person comfortable.

Clinical nursing

  • Ryle's tube and PEG feeding, and urinary catheter care.
  • Tracheostomy care, suctioning and oxygen therapy as prescribed.
  • Pressure sore prevention and wound care.
  • IV fluids or medicines when prescribed.

Support for the family

  • Explaining what the nurse is doing and why, in simple language.
  • Teaching family members safe ways to help with care if they wish.
  • Practical help in the final days, and calm guidance on whom to contact.

Who prescribes medicines in home palliative care?

All medicines, including pain relief, are prescribed by the patient's doctor or palliative care physician. Our nurses give medicines as prescribed, record each dose and observe how well it works, so the doctor can adjust the plan. Nurses do not change doses on their own. If you do not yet have a palliative care doctor, ask your treating hospital for a referral.

Who can benefit from palliative care at home?

  • People with advanced cancer who wish to be cared for at home.
  • Patients with advanced heart, lung, kidney or liver disease.
  • People with progressive neurological conditions, or after a severe stroke.
  • Bedridden patients with complex needs such as tube feeding or a tracheostomy.
  • Families who need nursing help to care for a loved one in their final weeks.

How palliative care at home begins

  1. Talk to us. Call or WhatsApp and tell us about the illness, current symptoms and the care plan.
  2. Share the plan. Send the latest discharge summary, prescription and any palliative care advice.
  3. Assessment. We discuss the nursing tasks, the family's wishes and the home setting.
  4. Matched nurse. We assign a nurse experienced in comfort care and agree the shift pattern.
  5. Care and communication. The nurse keeps a daily record and stays in touch with the doctor about any change.
  6. Equipment. If needed, we can supply a hospital bed, air mattress, oxygen concentrator or suction machine.

Frequently asked questions

Is palliative care only for the last days of life?

No. Palliative care can begin at any stage of a serious illness and can run alongside other treatment. Its aim is comfort and quality of life.

What is the difference between palliative care and hospice care?

Hospice care is palliative care for people in the final stage of life. Palliative care is broader and can be given earlier, together with treatment.

Can pain be managed well at home?

Often yes, when a doctor prescribes and reviews the pain plan and a nurse gives the medicines on time and reports how well they work.

Do you provide a palliative care doctor?

Our nurses work with the patient's own doctor or palliative care team.

Can you care for a patient with a feeding tube or tracheostomy?

Yes. We assign nurses experienced in tube feeding, tracheostomy care and suctioning.

Is 24-hour palliative nursing available?

Yes, through a live-in nurse or two 12-hour shifts, depending on how much active care is needed at night.

Can family members still be involved in care?

Absolutely. Many families want to help, and the nurse can show you safe ways to do so.

What should we do if the patient's condition changes suddenly?

The nurse will contact the doctor and follow the plan agreed in advance. Discuss with the doctor ahead of time what you would want to happen in an emergency.

How much does palliative care at home cost?

The cost depends on the nursing hours and any equipment required. We share the details after the assessment.
