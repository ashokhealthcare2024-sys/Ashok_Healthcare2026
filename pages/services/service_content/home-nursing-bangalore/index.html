What is home nursing?

Home nursing is skilled clinical care given by a qualified nurse in the patient's home instead of a hospital ward. A home nurse can give prescribed injections and IV fluids, dress wounds, manage catheters and feeding tubes, monitor vital signs and recognise when a patient needs a doctor. It suits patients who are medically stable but still need professional nursing.

For many families in Bangalore, the need arises suddenly. A father is discharged with a urinary catheter and a course of IV antibiotics. A mother comes home from hospital with a feeding tube. An older relative is bedridden after a fall and is developing pressure sores. In each case, the family can manage meals and company, but the clinical tasks need a trained hand.

Ashok Healthcare arranges nurses from our base in Yeshwanthpur. Before we assign anyone, our coordinator reads the discharge summary and prescriptions, asks about the home and the family's routine, and then matches a nurse whose training fits the tasks involved.

Nursing care we provide at home

Clinical procedures are provided according to the patient's prescriptions and nursing instructions.

Medicines and injections

  • Prescribed intramuscular, subcutaneous and intravenous injections.
  • IV fluid and IV antibiotic administration through an existing cannula, as prescribed.
  • Medicines given and recorded on time, with doses checked against the prescription.
  • Insulin administration and blood glucose checks.

Wound, skin and pressure care

  • Surgical and chronic wound dressing as directed by the doctor.
  • Prevention and dressing of pressure sores (bedsores), with regular repositioning.
  • Stoma care and skin care for bedridden patients.

Tubes, catheters and airways

  • Urinary (Foley) catheter care and changes as prescribed.
  • Ryle's tube (nasogastric) and PEG tube feeding.
  • Tracheostomy care and suctioning by nurses trained in airway care.
  • Nebulisation and oxygen therapy as prescribed.

Monitoring and reporting

  • Temperature, pulse, blood pressure, oxygen saturation and blood sugar recorded on a daily chart.
  • Intake and output monitoring where the doctor requests it.
  • Early reporting of warning signs to the family and the doctor.

12-hour and 24-hour home nurses: which do you need?

A 12-hour nurse works either a day or a night shift. This suits patients who need clinical tasks at set times, such as morning injections and dressings, while family members cover the other half of the day. A 24-hour live-in nurse stays at the home and is available around the clock, with agreed rest breaks. Live-in care suits patients who are bedridden, have a tracheostomy or need care through the night. For patients who need active nursing all 24 hours, two nurses working 12-hour shifts are safer than one live-in nurse.

How is a home nurse different from a caregiver?

A home nurse holds a nursing qualification (GNM or B.Sc Nursing) and performs clinical procedures. A caregiver, also called an attendant or caretaker, helps with bathing, feeding, toileting, moving and companionship but does not perform clinical procedures. Many families use a caregiver for daily needs and book a nurse only for clinical tasks. Contact our coordinator if you need help deciding which type of support fits your family.

Who may need a nurse at home?

  • Patients discharged early who still need IV antibiotics or injections.
  • Bedridden patients with catheters, feeding tubes or pressure sores.
  • People with a tracheostomy who need airway care at home.
  • Patients with diabetes who need insulin and close glucose monitoring.
  • Older adults who need daily clinical checks after a hospital stay.
  • Patients receiving long-term or palliative care at home.

If the patient's condition is unstable, for example frequent oxygen drops or changing blood pressure, the treating doctor may advise continued hospital care or a home ICU setup instead.

Frequently asked questions

What qualifications do your home nurses have?

Our nurses hold a GNM diploma or a B.Sc Nursing degree. For tracheostomy or critical care cases, we assign nurses with experience in those procedures.

Can a home nurse give IV fluids and injections?

Yes, when they are prescribed by a doctor. The nurse follows the written prescription for the drug, dose and timing, and records each dose on the care chart.

Is a 24-hour nurse awake all night?

A live-in nurse is available through the night but needs rest breaks. If the patient needs active care throughout the night, we recommend a dedicated night nurse or two 12-hour shifts.

How much does home nursing cost in Bangalore?

Charges depend on the nurse's qualification, the shift length and the clinical tasks. Contact our coordinator for an assessment and current charges.

Will the same nurse come every day?

We aim to keep the same nurse for continuity. If a change is needed, we brief the new nurse on the care plan before they start.

Who supervises the nurse?

A supervisor from our team monitors the care, reviews the daily chart and is your point of contact for any concerns.

What does the family need to provide?

Usually a place to rest for live-in nurses, access to clean water and a space for supplies. Medicines and consumables are normally bought by the family as prescribed.

What happens if the patient's condition worsens?

The nurse will inform you and the treating doctor immediately and follow the emergency plan agreed at the start. In an emergency, call 108 or go to the nearest hospital.

Do you provide nurses outside North Bangalore?

Often yes, depending on availability. Call us with the address and we will confirm.
