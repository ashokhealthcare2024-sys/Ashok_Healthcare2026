What is stroke rehabilitation at home?

Stroke rehabilitation at home is a programme of therapy delivered at the patient's home to help them regain movement, balance and independence after a stroke. A physiotherapist works on strength, walking, balance and arm and hand function through repeated, goal-directed practice, and teaches the family how to help safely between sessions.

A stroke can affect movement on one side of the body (hemiplegia), balance, sensation, speech, swallowing and memory. Recovery is different for every person. Many people make the most noticeable progress in the first months after a stroke, but improvement can continue for much longer with continued practice.

Home rehabilitation lets the patient practise exactly what they need: getting out of their own bed, walking to their own bathroom, climbing the stairs at home. Our physiotherapists also apply the same approach to other neurological conditions, such as Parkinson's disease, spinal cord injury, Guillain-Barré syndrome and recovery after brain surgery.

Neuro rehabilitation we provide at home

Movement and walking

  • Bed mobility, sitting balance and safe transfers.
  • Standing balance and walking practice, with or without aids.
  • Stair practice when appropriate.

Arm and hand function

  • Exercises to improve shoulder, arm and hand movement.
  • Task practice such as reaching, grasping and using utensils.
  • Positioning of the weak arm to protect the shoulder.

Muscle tone and joint care

  • Stretching and positioning to manage stiffness (spasticity).
  • Joint movement exercises to prevent contractures.

Daily living and family training

  • Practice of everyday activities such as dressing and washing.
  • Teaching family members and attendants how to assist safely.
  • Advice on walking aids, wheelchairs and home changes.

Does Ashok Healthcare provide speech or occupational therapy?

Speech, language and swallowing difficulties should be assessed by a qualified speech-language pathologist, and occupational therapy can help with daily living skills. Our physiotherapists will advise when these specialists are needed.

Who benefits from neuro rehabilitation at home?

  • Stroke survivors discharged from hospital who still need help to move or walk.
  • People with Parkinson's disease whose balance or walking is worsening.
  • Patients with spinal cord injury or nerve conditions after hospital treatment.
  • Patients who find clinic visits exhausting or impractical.
  • Families who want guidance on helping a loved one safely at home.

How stroke rehabilitation at home is arranged

  1. Contact us and share the discharge summary and any therapy notes from hospital.
  2. Assessment. The physiotherapist assesses movement, balance, tone and the home setup, and agrees goals with the patient and family.
  3. Plan and sessions. We suggest session frequency; early recovery often benefits from frequent sessions. Each session builds on the last, with exercises for the family or attendant to support between visits.
  4. Review. Goals are reviewed regularly and shared with the treating doctor where appropriate.

Frequently asked questions

When should rehabilitation start after a stroke?

Rehabilitation usually starts in hospital once the patient is medically stable, and should continue after discharge. Follow your doctor's advice on when home therapy can begin.

Can stroke rehabilitation be done effectively at home?

Yes, for many patients. Home rehabilitation allows practice of real daily tasks in the patient's own environment. Patients with complex needs may also need input from other specialists.

How long does stroke recovery take?

Recovery varies widely. Many people improve most in the first months, but progress can continue for a long time with regular practice. We cannot predict or promise a specific outcome.

How often should sessions happen?

Early on, more frequent sessions are often helpful. The physiotherapist will suggest a frequency after assessment and adjust it as you progress.

Can you help with speech or swallowing problems?

These need assessment by a qualified speech-language pathologist.

Do you treat Parkinson's disease?

Yes. Physiotherapy can help with balance, walking and everyday movements for people with Parkinson's disease.

Can family members learn the exercises?

Yes. We encourage family members and attendants to learn safe ways to help, so practice continues between sessions.

How much does neuro rehabilitation at home cost?

Costs depend on the frequency and number of sessions. Contact us for an assessment.

Can you also provide a nurse or attendant?

Yes. Many stroke patients need nursing or attendant care alongside therapy, and we can arrange both.
