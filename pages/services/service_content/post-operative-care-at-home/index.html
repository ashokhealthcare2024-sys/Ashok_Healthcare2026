What does post-operative care at home include?

Post-operative care at home is nursing support for a patient who has had surgery and has been discharged but still needs clinical attention. It usually covers wound and dressing care, management of drains or catheters, giving prescribed medicines and injections, pain and comfort support, safe mobility and watching for signs of infection or other complications.

Hospital stays after surgery are often short. A patient may go home after a hernia repair, a gall bladder removal, a cardiac procedure or a joint replacement while stitches are still in place and a drain may still be attached. Families are handed a discharge summary with a list of medicines and instructions, and suddenly they are responsible for tasks they have never done.

Our nurses take on those tasks. The surgeon's instructions remain the plan; the nurse makes sure they are carried out correctly, consistently and with a record the surgeon can review at the follow-up appointment.

Nursing care after surgery

Every task follows the surgeon's discharge instructions and the patient's prescribed care plan.

Wound and dressing care

  • Dressing changes using clean or sterile technique, at the frequency the surgeon advises.
  • Observation of the incision for redness, swelling, discharge or opening.
  • Stitch or staple removal when the surgeon has asked for it to be done at home.

Drains, catheters and tubes

  • Emptying and measuring surgical drain output and recording it.
  • Urinary catheter care, and removal when advised.
  • Feeding tube care for patients after abdominal or head and neck surgery.

Medicines and pain support

  • Prescribed oral medicines, injections and IV antibiotics on time.
  • Anticoagulant injections (for example, after joint replacement) as prescribed.
  • Pain scores recorded so the doctor can adjust pain relief if needed.

Movement, breathing and comfort

  • Help with the first walks, transfers from bed to chair and toileting.
  • Breathing and coughing exercises taught by the hospital team, encouraged regularly.
  • Repositioning to protect the skin of patients who spend long periods in bed.

Which warning signs will the nurse watch for?

Our nurses are trained to notice changes that need the surgeon's attention, such as fever, increasing pain, a wound that becomes red, hot or starts to discharge, a sudden increase in drain output, calf pain or swelling, breathlessness, or confusion. When they see these signs, they inform the family and the treating doctor straight away.

Which patients benefit from nursing at home after surgery?

  • Patients discharged with a drain, catheter or complex dressing.
  • Older adults returning home after hip fracture surgery or joint replacement.
  • Patients after cardiac, abdominal, urological or spine surgery who need close observation.
  • People who live alone or whose family members work long hours.
  • Patients with diabetes, whose wounds may need closer monitoring.

For patients who need help to walk again or regain joint movement, nursing can be combined with physiotherapy at home. Ask our coordinator about orthopaedic rehabilitation at home for recovery after joint replacement and fracture surgery.

How post-operative care at home is arranged

  1. Before discharge, call or WhatsApp us with the expected discharge date and the type of surgery. Planning a day or two ahead allows us to have a nurse and any equipment ready.
  2. Share the discharge summary and prescriptions. These form the care plan.
  3. Assessment. Our coordinator confirms the tasks, frequency and shift length needed.
  4. Equipment, if required. A hospital bed, wheelchair or commode can be delivered before the patient arrives home.
  5. Nursing begins with the agreed visits or shifts, and every dressing, dose and observation is recorded.
  6. Step down. As the patient recovers, care is reduced, usually from a full shift to shorter visits, and then stopped when the surgeon confirms it is no longer needed.

Frequently asked questions

How soon after surgery can home nursing start?

It can start on the day of discharge if we know the plan in advance. Contact us a day or two before discharge where possible.

How often does a surgical wound need dressing?

This depends on the type of surgery and dressing used. Some dressings stay in place for several days; others need daily changes. The nurse follows the surgeon's written instructions.

Do I need a full-time nurse after surgery?

Not always. Some patients need a 12-hour or 24-hour nurse for the first few days and then only visits for dressing and injections. The assessment helps decide what is enough.

Can the nurse remove stitches or staples at home?

Only when the surgeon has advised removal at home and on the date they specify.

What if the wound looks infected?

The nurse will document what they see and contact the treating doctor promptly. Do not apply creams or start antibiotics without the doctor's advice.

Can you help after a knee or hip replacement?

Yes. Nursing covers wound care, medicines and safe transfers, and our physiotherapists can provide the exercise programme the surgeon recommends.

Who buys the dressings and medicines?

Usually the family purchases medicines and dressing supplies as prescribed. Our nurse will tell you what to keep in stock.

How much does post-operative nursing cost?

The cost depends on the shift length and tasks involved. We share the charges after the assessment.

Is home care safe straight after major surgery?

Home care is suitable once the surgical team has decided the patient is stable enough for discharge. If the team advises continued hospital or step-down care, that advice should be followed.

Planning a discharge after surgery?

Send us the expected discharge date and the surgeon's instructions. We will arrange the nurse, and any equipment, so the patient comes home to a ready room.
